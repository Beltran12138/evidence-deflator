How We Grade Hyperliquid Traders: The S–F Methodology

Understanding how we grade Hyperliquid traders is the difference between copying a genuinely skilled wallet and chasing a lucky streak that's about to revert. Every rated wallet on LiquidWhales earns a single letter grade — S, A, B, C, D, or F — built from on-chain trade history and measured net of trading fees, never gross ROI.

This page is the cornerstone of our methodology. We walk through exactly which metrics feed the grade, how they're weighted, why a wallet with too thin a record shows as "U" (unrated) rather than F, and why a dormant wallet isn't automatically downgraded to F. No black boxes — Hyperliquid's transparency means you can audit the same fills we do.

Key takeaways
  • Every grade is computed net of trading fees — not gameable gross ROI.
  • Five pillars feed the S–F grade: win rate, risk-adjusted return, drawdown, longevity, and round-trip sample size.
  • An F means a net loss after fees or a drawdown above 50%; wallets with too little data to vouch for show as U (unrated), not F.
  • Dormant wallets aren't auto-downgraded to F (S-tier does require a trade in the last 5 days); recency is shown separately from skill.
  • Hyperliquid's on-chain settlement lets you audit every fill behind a grade yourself.

Why net of fees is the foundation

Gross ROI is the single most gameable number in trading. A wallet can post a huge headline return while bleeding out on taker fees, funding payments, and slippage — leaving copiers with a fraction of the advertised gain, or a loss. That's why every metric in our grade is computed net of trading fees, charged on every fill the way your account would actually pay them.

On Hyperliquid this matters even more than on a spot exchange. Perps charge funding every hour, and a trader who holds the wrong side of a crowded trade pays for it continuously. We subtract the trading fee charged on every fill, opens included, before any performance statistic is calculated, so a grade reflects after-fee edge, not vanity numbers.

  • Taker and maker fees deducted per fill
  • Funding payments not deducted — weigh them separately
  • Realized closed PnL, not open mark-to-market (S-tier counts only round-trips that reconcile)

The five pillars behind every grade

A letter grade is a composite. No single lucky trade earns an S, and a bad week alone doesn't earn an F — only a net loss after fees or a drawdown above 50% does. We combine five pillars, each capturing a different way a trader can be skilled — or fragile.

1. Win rate (with context)

The share of closing trades that ended in profit, net of fees. Win rate alone is misleading — a 90% win rate paired with rare catastrophic losses is a martingale waiting to blow up — so we always read it alongside the size of wins versus losses.

2. Risk-adjusted return (Sharpe-style)

Return scaled by volatility. A trader who grinds steady gains scores higher than one who posts the same total return through wild, stomach-churning swings, because the smooth path is far more copyable.

3. Maximum drawdown

The worst peak-to-trough decline. Drawdown is what actually makes copiers panic and disconnect at the bottom, so deep or frequent drawdowns pull a grade down hard regardless of headline return.

4. Longevity

How long the wallet has been trading with real size through different market regimes. Surviving a funding squeeze, a liquidation cascade, and a chop-filled range is evidence; a two-week hot streak is not.

5. Round-trip count (sample size)

How many completed trades back the statistics. A handful of trades can't distinguish skill from noise, so a wallet needs at least 10 closing trades to be rated at all, and 20 completed round-trips before it is eligible for S.

What S through F actually means

The grade is ordinal — it ranks wallets relative to a demanding standard, not a curve that hands out a few S's no matter what.

  • S — elite: strong net-of-fees returns, controlled drawdown, long track record, and a large round-trip sample. These are the wallets that populate our smart-money cohorts.
  • A / B — consistently good, with one pillar slightly weaker (e.g. great returns but a shorter history).
  • C / D — mixed: real activity but unremarkable risk-adjusted results, or a drawdown that gives us pause.
  • F — rated, but lost money after fees or blew a drawdown above 50%; see below. Too-thin records show as U (unrated), not F.

Grades update as new fills land on-chain, so a wallet's letter is a living read, not a one-time stamp.

What F really means (and why unrated isn't F)

This is the most misread part of the system. An F is reserved for rated wallets (at least 10 closing trades) that fail in one of two ways:

  • Net losers — negative returns after trading fees across their realized record.
  • Blown drawdowns — a peak-to-trough drawdown above 50%, even if the wallet is net-profitable overall (unless it has zero losing closes).

In other words, F means "this trader lost money after fees" or "this trader blew a drawdown above 50%." Wallets we don't have enough evidence on yet, with fewer than 10 closing trades, aren't graded F at all: they show as U (unrated). We won't assign a flattering grade on thin data, and you shouldn't copy a wallet on hope. As more trades accumulate, a U wallet earns a letter once the sample supports it.

Why dormant isn't the same as bad

A wallet that stops trading hasn't suddenly lost its skill. A proven trader might step aside during a regime they dislike — that's discipline, not failure — so we don't punish inactivity by reflexively slashing an A–D grade.

What we do instead is surface recency clearly so you can make your own call. A high grade earned over years remains informative, but you'll see when the last fill landed, and S-tier itself requires a trade within the last 5 days, so a paused S wallet drops out of S until it trades again. The grade answers "was this trader skilled?"; recency answers "are they trading right now?" — two separate questions you should keep separate.

You can inspect both, trade by trade, on any wallet's page in the graded whale tracker, where closed round-trips are shown net of fees alongside the live grade.

Auditing the grade yourself

Because Hyperliquid settles on-chain, every fill that feeds a grade is publicly verifiable. We don't ask you to trust a number — we encourage you to check it.

  • Open a wallet on the whale tracker and review its closed trades net of fees.
  • Compare its placement on the net-of-fees leaderboard against any gross-ROI list you've seen elsewhere.
  • Cross-reference crowd positioning with our consensus signals and per-coin smart-money flow before committing capital.

The grade is a starting filter, not a recommendation. Copy trading carries real risk — past performance, however rigorously measured, does not guarantee future results, and none of this is financial advice. Size positions accordingly and use native stops and the kill-switch.

FAQ

What metrics decide a Hyperliquid trader's grade?
Five pillars: win rate, risk-adjusted (Sharpe-style) return, maximum drawdown, longevity across market regimes, and round-trip sample size. All are computed net of trading fees, never on gross ROI.
Does an F grade always mean the trader lost money?
No. F marks a rated wallet that either lost money after trading fees or suffered a peak-to-trough drawdown above 50%, so some F wallets are net-profitable but blew a big drawdown. Wallets with fewer than 10 closing trades aren't graded F at all: they show as U (unrated) until enough net-of-fees trades accumulate.
Why grade net of fees instead of ROI?
Gross ROI ignores taker/maker fees and hourly perp funding, which can erase most of a headline return on Hyperliquid. Grading net of fees strips out the trading-fee drag a copier would actually pay; funding isn't deducted, so weigh it separately.
Is a dormant whale automatically downgraded?
Not to F. Pausing during an unfavorable regime is often discipline, not failure, so inactivity doesn't slash an A–D grade, but S-tier requires a trade within the last 5 days. We surface last-trade recency separately so you can weigh it before copying.
Can I verify a grade myself?
Yes. Hyperliquid settles on-chain, so every fill is public. You can audit any wallet's closed round-trips net of fees on the whale tracker and compare its standing on the net-of-fees leaderboard.
