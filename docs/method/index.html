Method

Plain arithmetic on inputs anyone can state

Every number on this site can be recomputed from public data with the scripts in the repository. Nothing depends on a model of ours, and nothing needs the strategy’s code.

1 · Open-book months

Split the test window at the model’s published training cutoff. Months on or before the cutoff cannot be ruled out as seen: the model may already know what happened. Months after it are clean.

window 2020-01 → 2025-06, cutoff 2024-10 66 months = 58 open-book + 8 clean

Cutoffs come only from the provider’s own documentation. Where none is published, we say so and do not estimate one. An earlier cutoff always makes a result look better, so it is the input that most needs a source.

2 · Clean months needed

A result of annualised Sharpe S reaches t = 2 after T years, where t ≈ S × √T (Lo, 2002):

months needed = (t / S)² × 12 Sharpe 2 → 12 Sharpe 1 → 48 Sharpe 0.5 → 192

If the clean segment is shorter than this, the backtest is underpowered: it has not been shown to fail, only not shown to work. The i.i.d. assumption makes this a floor; autocorrelated returns need longer.

3 · The bar after selection

A result picked as the best of N attempts is the maximum of N draws. We raise the bar with a one-sided Bonferroni correction that keeps a single attempt at exactly t = 2:

α = 1 − Φ(2) ≈ 2.3%, one-sided bar(N) = Φ⁻¹(1 − α / N) N = 1 → 2.00 N = 41 → 3.26 N = 23,376 → 4.76

Bonferroni assumes independent attempts. Correlated variants (one owner’s parameter grid, teams on the same model) make it conservative. We say so rather than switch to a correction whose assumptions a reader cannot check; the library accepts a measured effective number of trials when one exists.

4 · Leaderboards

For each entry we take the published equity history, keep the last value of each day, and compute daily returns. Crypto boards annualise with 365 days, equity calendars with 252.

Sharpe = mean(daily return) / sd(daily return) × √(days per year) t = Sharpe × √(years of data)

“Nobody is significant” says little on its own, so each board also gets a date: how many months of clean, forward history the top entry would need to clear the best-of-N bar, if its true Sharpe were S.

months to a winner = (bar(N) / S)² × 12

We report it at S = 5 and S = 2, and at the closest entry’s observed Sharpe. These are conditional statements, not forecasts.

5 · Wallets: returns, deposits and the bar

A wallet’s weekly return is the change in the PnL Hyperliquid records for the account over the week, divided by the account value at the start of that week. Hyperliquid’s PnL history excludes deposits and withdrawals, so money moving in or out changes the account value but is never counted as a gain or a loss. It does change the denominator for the weeks after it: a return is always measured against the money that was in the account when the week began. Weeks that start with under $1,000 in the account are skipped, because a small base turns ordinary dollar swings into extreme returns; when most of a wallet’s PnL falls in skipped weeks, the page says so.

t is computed from those weekly returns with a Student’s t correction for the record’s effective length, then put on the normal scale. A wallet found on a leaderboard of N accounts needs bar(N) from section 3. That bar treats the N accounts as independent; accounts that trade alike are fewer independent bets, which would lower it (about 2.6 for Hyperliquid by our rough estimate, an order of magnitude rather than a measurement). We show both.

Not the Deflated Sharpe Ratio. Bailey & López de Prado’s DSR deflates a Sharpe ratio for the number of trials using the spread of Sharpe ratios across those trials, and adjusts for skew and fat tails. We do something simpler: a small-sample t and a Bonferroni bar for best-of-N, which needs only N and is conservative when the trials are correlated. The name is about deflating evidence, not a claim to implement DSR.

What a wallet check counts: perps and the spot account, including HIP-4 outcome contracts; not HyperEVM. Vaults are scored on the vault’s own PnL, before the leader’s profit share, and labelled as vaults on the boards.

Drawdown reading. Next to t, the wallet page shows how far the record is below its own peak, as the sum of the weekly returns since the peak, and the share of time a record with the same measured weekly mean m and variance s² would spend at least that far down: exp(−2m(d + 0.5826 s)/s²) for a drawdown d. That is the stationary drawdown of a random walk with drift, with Siegmund’s correction for sampling once a week; without it the share is up to 1.6 times too high at a Sharpe of 3. The edge is measured on the same record, so the reading leans toward calling a drawdown unusual.

Principles

  • Sources are public and fetched on a stated date. Numbers are recomputed, not copied.
  • “Cannot be ruled out as seen” is the strongest claim made about a model’s training data. Never “contaminated”.
  • A missing input is a finding, not a verdict on the project.
  • Findings state what the numbers can and cannot support. No judgement of intent, no investment advice.
  • The same rule for every board and every claim. No grading on a curve.
  • Corrections are published. If you think a number is wrong, open an issue.

What this does not do

  • It does not say any strategy or model is unskilled. It says the evidence cannot yet tell skill from luck.
  • It does not detect leakage inside a model; it bounds where leakage cannot be excluded.
  • It uses only what a project or board publishes.
  • The Hyperliquid wallet check counts what Hyperliquid records for the account: perps and spot, including HIP-4 outcome contracts. Activity on HyperEVM is not included.
  • A wallet check measures the evidence in a record, not what copying it would return. A copier trades later and at another size; the shorter the holding period, the more the delay costs (one manager’s example: a strategy that loses 20% of its PnL when delayed by 15 minutes, source).
  • The tail notes read the shape of past weeks. A strategy that sells tail risk and scales its positions to recent volatility can keep that shape ordinary until a large loss and, scaled down, around it.
